### Stage 4: Mock client call · 45 min · Daniel Okafor
**Purpose:** See how the candidate handles an upset clinic owner, live.

**Opening (say this):** "Hi [name], I'm Daniel, a Senior CSM at Fernhill. You'll have 5 minutes to read a short scenario. Then I'll play an upset clinic owner for about 20 minutes, and we'll finish with a few questions."

**Timing:** 5 minutes to read, 20 minutes of role-play, 15 minutes of questions, 5 minutes for the candidate's questions.

**Scenario card (the same for every candidate, pasted into the call chat):**
> You're the CSM for Lakeside Physio, a clinic with three physiotherapists. Yesterday, Fernhill's reminder texts went out with the wrong appointment times, and four patients turned up at the wrong time this morning. Our product team has found the cause and is testing a fix. They don't know yet when it will be live. The owner, Sam Carter, has asked for a call. The clinic's contract renews next month.

**How Daniel plays Sam (the same way every time):**
- Opens angry: "My front desk spent the whole morning apologising. This is the second time this year."
- Asks once: "Can you promise this won't happen again?"
- Says once: "Honestly, I'm not sure we'll renew."
- Calms down only if the candidate listens, owns the problem and gives a clear next step with a time. Stays frustrated if they get defensive, blame someone else or promise a date they don't have.

## Remote checks

### A. Timezone overlap conversation
**Who and when:** Hamza, in the recruiter screen.

**Say this:** "Our overlap with the Austin team is 9am to 1pm US Central, Monday to Friday. In Pakistan that's 7pm to 11pm from mid-March to early November, and 8pm to midnight the rest of the year, because US clocks change and Pakistan's don't. You'd start on 1 December, so you'd begin on 8pm to midnight. How would that fit your life, week after week?"

**Follow-ups, the same for everyone:**
- "Have you worked hours like these before? What helped?"
- "How would you hand over to the Austin team at the end of your day?"
- "Is there anything about these hours you'd like to ask us?"

**Listen for:** Honesty about the real cost and a plan that works week after week. "It'll be hard, and here's how I'd manage it" is a good answer. Note only the plan. If they share personal details, don't follow up on them and don't write them down.

### C. Remote-readiness conversation
**Who and when:** Hamza, in the recruiter screen, the same way for every candidate.

**Ask every candidate the same questions:**
1. "Where do you usually work from?"
2. "What's your plan if your power or internet goes down during a client call?"
3. "How would you let the clinic and the team know?"

**A good answer** names a backup and a habit. A backup could be a UPS (a battery that keeps a laptop and router running in a power cut), a mobile hotspot or a co-working space. A habit could be messaging the clinic and the team straight away and offering a new time.

**Fairness rules:**
- Judge the plan, not the setup. Power cuts and patchy internet happen in many places. The plan is what matters.
- Never judge the home, the background, an accent, video quality or the connection.
- If a call drops, reschedule. It's never a mark against the candidate.
- Never ask about family, children, marriage or religion. Not even as small talk.
- **What Fernhill offers:** Not yet agreed. Rachel to confirm before the first screen, so Hamza can tell every candidate the same thing.
