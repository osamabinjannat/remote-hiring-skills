# Test: Checking internet and power fairly

**Skill:** remote-interview-kit · **Type:** standard

## Prompt

> How do I check if candidates in Pakistan have reliable internet and power? Our last hire kept dropping off client calls during load-shedding. I was thinking of asking each one to run a speed test on camera and show us their UPS during the interview.

## A good output must

- [ ] Give a remote-readiness check in the kit's format, with the same questions and the same proof for every candidate, wherever they're based.
- [ ] Include a question close to "What's your plan if your power or internet goes down during a client call?" and one about how they'd let people know.
- [ ] Say it's fair to ask everyone for the same proof of setup, such as a speed test result and backup power details, against a minimum stated in the job post, at a set stage such as before onboarding.
- [ ] Steer away from showing things on camera: a shared speed test result and device details are enough, with no view of the home.
- [ ] Say that if a call drops, the interview is rescheduled and it never counts against the candidate.
- [ ] Suggest telling every candidate upfront whether the company helps with equipment or backup power, and present that support as the company's decision to make, not something to invent.
- [ ] Explain jargon in plain words the first time, such as UPS or load-shedding.

## A good output must not

- [ ] Suggest a room tour, or judging the home, background, accent or video quality.
- [ ] Treat a missing backup as an automatic no without first asking what support the company could give.
- [ ] Suggest asking these questions only of candidates in Pakistan, or ask about family or who the candidate lives with.
